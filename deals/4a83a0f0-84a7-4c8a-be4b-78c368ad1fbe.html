The Flood Zone — Read This Before Anything Else

Zone AE means a FEMA special flood hazard area with a published Base Flood Elevation. Building is not prohibited, but it is governed, and the governing is real.

What to establish before writing an offer

  • The Base Flood Elevation at this specific parcel
  • Whether any portion of your proposed building area falls within the regulatory floodway, as opposed to the flood fringe — this is the single most important question on the lot. Floodway restrictions are far more severe than flood fringe restrictions and in many cases effectively preclude a structure
  • The elevation requirement for the lowest floor, including any freeboard the local ordinance adds above BFE
  • The floodplain development permit process and what it will cost in time
  • What an elevated foundation does to your build budget and to your finished product's marketability
  • Flood insurance cost for the eventual homeowner, which affects what your buyer can afford to pay for the house

Mecklenburg County maintains its own community flood mapping, including a Community Encroachment Area layer, separate from the federal FEMA data. Checking the FEMA map alone is not sufficient diligence here — pull both, and talk to Charlotte-Mecklenburg Storm Water Services directly before you commit.

Everything above is why this lot is priced where it is. None of it is hidden.

The Zoning Question

The parcel is zoned N1-A under Charlotte's current Unified Development Ordinance, having previously been R-3 under the legacy code.

Confirm legal nonconforming lot status. Reported frontage and width may not meet current N1-A dimensional standards, which is common for older platted lots carried into a new code. A lot that is legally nonconforming is generally buildable to the standards in place when it was created — a lot that is not can be a very different conversation. Get this answered in writing by Charlotte Planning before you close.
