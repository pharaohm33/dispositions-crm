The Opportunity

Two separate deeded lots in The Nations, and one is already vacant.

Most two-build pitches sell buyers a hope: one parcel, one tax card, and a promise that Metro will approve a split down the road. This is not that. These two parcels are separate parcels of record — two parcel numbers, two legal descriptions, two deeds, recorded in 1963 and 1964. The Davidson County Assessor already classifies one parcel as vacant residential land. The split happened sixty years ago; no subdivision approval is required.

Both parcels sit under RS3.75 zoning with the Urban Zoning Overlay. RS3.75 requires a minimum 3,750 square foot lot. One parcel is 5,148 square feet, the other is 5,401 square feet — both clear the minimum with room to spare.

Davidson County appraises the two parcels at $478,000 combined for 2026. The land value alone, before counting the existing structure, is $324,000. The ask is $275,000 — below the county's own combined land value, in The Nations, on a street where new construction has closed at $850,000.

Exit Strategies

  • Scrape and build two: demo the 1930 structure on the improved parcel, build new on both parcels, two finished exits on one acquisition.
  • Build one, hold one: the vacant parcel is ready for a permit set. Build there first, land-bank the improved parcel or rent the existing house while you go.
  • Renovate and build: keep the 1930 bungalow as a rehab or rental, put new construction on the vacant parcel, sell separately.
  • Split and flip the dirt: two deeded parcels means you can resell them individually to two different builders without touching Metro.
