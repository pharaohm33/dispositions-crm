The Story

This is a 2013 block-built, two-story home, 2,907 square feet, four bedrooms, three bathrooms, two-car garage, with a brand-new 2026 HVAC, and the seller reports it is mostly move-in ready. Seller's asking price is $275,990 — that works out to $95 per square foot on a home that is only thirteen years old.

Two comparable closings on the same street tell the story plainly. A 2014 build with 2,454 square feet — 453 square feet smaller than this home — closed at $485,000 in May 2026, going pending in just 13 days. Another home on the same street, 2,360 square feet, closed at $405,000 the following month. Same street, same builder era, same block construction — and this is the largest of the three at the lowest price by a wide margin.

How the ARV Pencils

Run it on price per foot and the picture gets simple. The comp set is trading between $172 and $198 a square foot, with comp reports putting the average at $185 to $186 per square foot. At 2,907 square feet, even $160 a foot puts this house at $465,000.

We are calling the ARV $450,000+, which works out to $155 a foot — below every comp on this list on a per-foot basis, and the number we are asking buyers to underwrite against.

One honest note on the $485,000 comp: that house has a pool and the subject does not. The comp report adjusts for the pool and still lands an adjusted value of $456,995. Even after removing the pool premium, that comp prints above our stated ARV, despite being 453 square feet smaller than the subject.

The subject is the largest home in this comp set. Buyers should run their own comps and confirm their own ARV.
