The Story

A 1998-built, three-bedroom, two-bath home on more than two-thirds of an acre in Bessemer City, priced at $148,000 against an after-repair value in the $243,000–$250,000 range.

The thing that makes this one work is what you do not have to do. Roof and HVAC are both under five years old. On a flip, those two line items are usually the first thing that eats your budget, and here they are already handled.

What is left is cosmetic: flooring throughout, interior paint, kitchen countertops and cabinet refinishing, a tub and vanity in the primary bath, lighting, and exterior pressure washing and cleanup. That is a scope you can run in weeks, not months.

The property is on well and septic, which on a 0.69-acre Gaston County parcel is normal and which should be inspected before closing.

The home is vacant at closing, available for showing by appointment, and this opportunity is first come, first served.

How to Read This Comp Set

Two comps, and here is what is strong about them and what is not.

What is strong: the Hephzibah Church Road comp is half a mile away, built the same year as the subject, three bed / two bath, no garage, no pool, and it closed at $245,000. On size, vintage, configuration, and distance, that is about as close a match as this market produces — it is the comp this exit rests on.

What to weigh: it is two comps, not five. The Lansdale Avenue comp is in Lincolnton, a different town 1.7 miles out, and it carries a third bathroom the subject does not have, which is part of why the model adjusted it down by $13,328. Both sales average nine months old, with Hephzibah closing in December 2025.

The subject's 0.69-acre lot matches the 0.70-acre comp average almost exactly, which is one fewer adjustment to argue about.

Pull your own Gaston County comps before setting your exit. Confirm construction type on the subject and on your comps while you are at it, and make sure you are comparing like to like.
