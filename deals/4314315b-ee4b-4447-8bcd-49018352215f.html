Condition — What We Know and What We Do Not

What we know: the roof is roughly ten years old, replaced by the owner rather than a flip-job patch, at a reported cost of about $12,000.

What we do not have: no condition report has been provided on the interior, exterior, foundation, electrical, plumbing, or HVAC. Records do not specify water source or sewer system. On rural Coryell County acreage, plan on a private well and a septic system until an inspection proves otherwise, and get both evaluated before closing. A failed septic system or a dry well is a five-figure problem and is the biggest single variable on a property like this.

Why the Barn Is the Deal

Two acres with no zoning and no HOA, with roughly 2,400 square feet of pole barn and a separate shop already standing. Consider what that supports: equipment storage, a contractor's yard, RV and boat storage in a stretch of Texas where that demand is real, a working shop for a trades buyer, covered space for livestock or hay, or a buyer who wants to run a business off the property while living on it — exactly what unrestricted rural acreage exists for. Price a new 40x60 pole barn today, then compare that to what this whole property costs.

Exit Strategies

  • Fix and hold: Rehab the house, hold the acreage and outbuildings — rural Coryell County rental demand runs off Fort Cavazos and the Copperas Cove corridor.
  • Fix and flip: Renovate and resell to a buyer who wants two unrestricted acres, a shop, and a barn with a house already on it.
  • Owner finance exit: Rural Texas acreage with a house, a barn, and a shop is one of the stronger owner-finance products in this market, and the property sits in Evant ISD.
  • Storage or trades income: No zoning on record and no HOA; buyer to confirm Coryell County requirements before commercial use.
  • Second dwelling: Buyer to confirm septic capacity, setback, and permitting with Coryell County before planning any additional structure.
